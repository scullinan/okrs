# Key Result Framework

Use this reference when coaching KRs in more depth.

## SMART

Each KR should be:

- Specific: Clear and unambiguous.
- Measurable: Progress can be tracked with numbers.
- Achievable: Realistic given the team's capacity.
- Relevant: Directly supports the Objective.
- Time-bound: Has a clear deadline.

Measurable does not mean "has a number somewhere." Prefer KRs that let the team inspect progress during the cycle. A final count that can be delayed until the last week is weaker than a metric that shows ongoing movement.

Ask for baseline, target, and timeframe whenever numbers are involved. If the baseline is unknown, mark that as a measurement risk and add a follow-up action to establish it quickly.

KRs can inherit the Objective deadline when it is clear, but be explicit when a KR has a unique timeframe or staged progress expectation.

## The Why Test

Ask:

> Why is this KR important for the Objective?

Use the answer to:

- Identify Output KRs: tasks, activities, or deliverables that contribute but do not directly measure success.
- Define Outcome KRs: results that reflect real progress toward the Objective.
- Assess Complementary KRs: missing conditions or guardrails needed for the Outcome KR to matter.
- Reveal hidden assumptions and alternative paths.

## Output KRs

Output KRs describe tasks, activities, deliverables, or binary completion. They are acceptable but not preferred.

Best practice:

- Do not let an Objective contain only Output KRs without challenge.
- Pair Output KRs with Outcome KRs that prove the work mattered.
- Suggest moving standalone outputs toward execution tracking only when useful, while keeping the coaching mostly in OKR language.

Binary KRs such as "Launch X by date" are Output KRs unless they directly express a measurable success state.

## Outcome KRs

Outcome KRs measure real movement toward the Objective. They are the most valuable KR type because they help the team learn whether its approach is working.

Strong Outcome KRs usually include:

- A meaningful metric.
- A current baseline or an explicit action to establish one.
- A target.
- A timeframe.
- Clear relevance to the Objective.
- Leading indicators where possible.

## Complementary KRs

Complementary KRs do not show direct progress by themselves. They protect the conditions needed for success or prevent a strong Outcome KR from becoming misleading.

Use Complementary KRs when the Why test exposes an assumption, dependency, resource condition, quality guardrail, or risk that must hold true.

Examples include maintaining budget, keeping quality above a threshold, preserving service reliability during a migration, or ensuring a required capacity condition remains true.

## KR Set Guidance

For a 1-3 month team OKR cycle, each Objective should usually have:

- Include at least 1 Outcome KR.
- Use up to 3 Outcome KRs as a guide.
- Add 1 to 2 Complementary KRs per Outcome KR when needed.
- Keep Output KRs only when they add execution context and are paired with Outcome KRs.

These are final acceptance guidelines, not brainstorming limits. During ideation, let the team generate more candidate KRs than will be accepted. Some candidates will be duplicates, weaker options, hidden assumptions, Output KRs that belong near execution, or ideas to defer.

For longer timeframes, such as a year, the final accepted KR set can be larger. Relax the numeric guidance only when the longer horizon genuinely requires it, and keep checking that the final set is still clear, owned, measurable, and usable for tracking progress.
